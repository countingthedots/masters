# Influencer marketing and Gen Z’s purchase intentions: a structural equation modeling approach


Kodiarasi Muthulingam 1 and Nixon Amirtharaj 2 

1 Department of Commerce, Faculty of Science and Humanities, SRM Institute of Science and
Technology, Vadapalani, Chennai, Tamil Nadu, India, [2] Department of Commerce (A&F), Faculty of
Science and Humanities, SRM Institute of Science and Technology, Vadapalani, Chennai, Tamil Nadu,

India


Introduction: Influencer marketing has become an important strategy for
shaping consumer attitudes and purchase behavior, particularly among
Generation Z consumers who are highly active on social media platforms.
Although previous studies have examined influencer credibility and parasocial
interaction separately, limited research has integrated influencer
characteristics and content attributes within a unified framework, especially in
emerging market contexts. This study investigates how influencer-related
factors influence Generation Z consumers’ purchase intention in India.
Methods: A quantitative cross-sectional research design was adopted. Data
were collected from 370 Generation Z respondents from Tier-1 metropolitan
cities in India who actively engage with influencer content on social media.
Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) was employed to examine the
relationships among influencer expertise, influencer passion, content quality,
frequency of exposure, engagement, trust, perception, and purchase intention.
Results: The findings indicate that trust and perception significantly influence
purchase intention and act as key mediating mechanisms. Influencer expertise
positively affects trust, while content quality positively affects perception.
Frequency of exposure negatively affects perception, suggesting diminishing
effectiveness of repeated influencer content. Engagement did not significantly
influence purchase intention and did not function as a mediator in this context.
Discussion: This study contributes to the influencer marketing literature by
integrating Source Credibility Theory and Parasocial Interaction Theory within
a single structural model and by providing empirical evidence from an
emerging market context. The findings offer practical implications for
marketers targeting Generation Z consumers by emphasizing the importance
of influencer credibility, authenticity, and high-quality content over excessive
exposure strategies.

## 1 Introduction

The proliferation of social media platforms has significantly
transformed the marketing landscape, enabling digital content
creators, commonly referred to as social media influencers, to
play a central role in shaping consumer attitudes and purchase
behavior. Despite the increasing adoption of influencer
marketing strategies, the mechanisms through which influencer

attributes and content characteristics translate into consumer

purchase intention remain insufficiently understood, particularly
among Generation Z consumers in emerging market contexts.

Generation Z, typically defined as individuals born between
the mid-1990s and early 2010s, represents a digitally native
cohort characterized by a strong preference for authenticity,
peer-based recommendations, and interactive engagement
(Fromm and Read, 2018; Williams et al., 2012). Social media
platforms such as Instagram and YouTube have become
dominant channels through which this cohort interacts with
brands and influencers. Prior research suggests that Generation
Z consumers are more likely to rely on influencers who
demonstrate relatability, authenticity, and consistent value
alignment, thereby enhancing the persuasive effectiveness of
influencer marketing (Djafarova and Trofimenko, 2019).
However, the extent to which specific influencer attributes and
content-related factors jointly influence consumer decisionmaking processes remains underexplored. The effectiveness of
influencer marketing can be explained through the integration
of key theoretical perspectives. Source Credibility Theory posits
that communicator characteristics, particularly expertise and
trustworthiness, influence audience attitudes and behavioral
responses (Ohanian, 1990). In this context, influencer expertise
serves as a cognitive cue that enhances perceived reliability and
reduces uncertainty in decision-making.

In contrast, Parasocial Interaction Theory explains how
audiences develop perceived emotional relationships with media
figures, resulting in feelings of familiarity, attachment, and social
connection (Horton and Wohl, 1956). These relationships are
particularly salient in social media environments, where
influencers are often perceived as relatable peers. Together, these
theories suggest that influencer attributes and content
characteristics shape psychological responses, including
engagement, trust, and perception, which subsequently influence
purchase intention. Although prior studies have examined
individual dimensions of influencer marketing, several research
gaps remain. First, existing research often treats influencers as a
homogeneous category and does not sufficiently distinguish
between specific influencer attributes, such as expertise and
passion, and content-related factors, such as content quality and
exposure frequency. Second, while psychological constructs such
as trust and engagement have been explored, limited studies
have examined the simultaneous mediating roles of engagement,
trust, and perception within a unified structural framework.
Third, empirical evidence focusing on Generation Z consumers
in the Indian context, particularly within Tier-1 metropolitan
cities characterized by high levels of digital engagement, remains
limited. Furthermore, lifestyle-oriented sectors such as fashion
and food, where visual appeal and emotional connection are
critical, have received relatively less attention in this context. To
address these gaps, this study develops and empirically tests a



theoretically grounded structural model that examines how
influencer characteristics (expertise and passion) and content
attributes (content quality and frequency of exposure) influence
Generation Z consumers’ purchase intention. The model further
investigates the mediating roles of engagement, trust, and
perception in explaining how influencer-related factors translate
into consumer behavior. Accordingly, the study addresses the
following research questions:


RQ1: How do influencer characteristics (expertise and
passion) and content attributes (content quality and exposure
frequency) influence Generation Z consumers’ purchase

intention?


RQ2: To what extent do engagement, trust, and perception
mediate the relationship between influencer marketing factors
and purchase intention?


This study adopts a quantitative cross-sectional research design
and employs Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) to examine
the relationships among the proposed constructs. By integrating
Source Credibility Theory and Parasocial Interaction Theory
within a unified framework, the study makes three key
contributions. First, it provides a more nuanced understanding
of influencer effectiveness by distinguishing between multiple
influencer attributes and content characteristics. Second, it
advances theoretical understanding by examining the differential
mediating roles of engagement, trust, and perception. Third, it
contributes empirical evidence from the Indian Generation
Z context, thereby extending the applicability of existing theories
to an emerging market setting. Overall, this study offers a
theoretically integrated and empirically grounded explanation of
how influencer marketing influences Generation Z consumers’
purchase intentions, with particular relevance to visually driven

sectors such as fashion.

## 2 Literature review


Understanding how Generation Z responds to influencer
marketing requires an integrated examination of persuasion
mechanisms and source-related cues within digital
environments. The **Elaboration Likelihood Model (ELM)** (Petty
and Cacioppo, 1986) provides a foundational framework for
explaining how individuals process persuasive messages through
either a central or peripheral route. In the context of social
media, where users are frequently exposed to large volumes of
content, Generation Z consumers are more likely to rely on
**peripheral cues** rather than engage in extensive cognitive
evaluation. These cues include influencer expertise, content
quality, and frequency of exposure, which function as heuristic
signals that shape attitudes and purchase intentions.

Building on this, Source Credibility Theory (SCT) emphasizes
that communicator characteristics, particularly expertise and
trustworthiness, play a critical role in influencing audience
responses (Ohanian, 1990). Influencer expertise serves as a
cognitive cue that enhances perceived reliability, while
trustworthiness reduces uncertainty in decision-making. Prior
research consistently demonstrates that higher levels of
perceived credibility lead to stronger consumer trust and more
favorable purchase intentions (Sokolova and Kefi, 2020). Thus,

credibility-related attributes operate as key drivers of persuasion in
influencer marketing contexts.

Complementing this perspective, Parasocial Interaction
Theory (PSI) explains how consumers develop perceived
emotional relationships with influencers (Horton and Wohl,
1956). Through repeated exposure and personalized content,
influencers create a sense of familiarity and intimacy, which
fosters emotional attachment and engagement. For Generation
Z consumers, who value relatability and authenticity, these
parasocial relationships play a crucial role in shaping how
influencer messages are interpreted and accepted.


2.1 Influencer expertise and passion


Influencer expertise and passion represent two distinct yet
complementary dimensions of influencer effectiveness. Expertise
refers to the perceived knowledge, competence, and experience
of the influencer within a specific domain (Ohanian, 1990).
Within the ELM framework, expertise functions as a peripheral
cue that signals credibility and reduces perceived risk, thereby
enhancing trust and influencing purchase intention.

In contrast, influencer passion reflects the emotional intensity
and enthusiasm expressed in content creation. Passion contributes
to perceived authenticity, which is particularly important for
Generation Z consumers. Influencers who demonstrate genuine
enthusiasm are more likely to be perceived as intrinsically
motivated, thereby strengthening emotional connection and
encouraging engagement (Audrezet et al., 2020). While expertise
primarily operates through cognitive evaluation, passion
functions through affective mechanisms, highlighting the dual
role of logic and emotion in influencer persuasion.


2.2 Content quality and frequency of
exposure


Content-related attributes play a significant role in shaping
consumer responses to influencer marketing. Content quality
refers to the perceived relevance, clarity, and aesthetic appeal of
influencer-generated content (De Veirman et al., 2017). Highquality content enhances message processing and positively
influences consumer evaluations, particularly through the central
route of persuasion when users engage more deeply with content.

Frequency of exposure, grounded in the mere exposure effect
(Zajonc, 1968), suggests that repeated exposure increases familiarity
and liking. However, in digital environments characterized by
content saturation, excessive exposure may lead to diminishing
returns. For Generation Z consumers, frequent exposure may shift
from reinforcing familiarity to generating fatigue, thereby
negatively affecting perceptions of authenticity and credibility.


2.3 Engagement, trust, and perception as
mediating mechanisms


Engagement, trust, and perception serve as key psychological
mechanisms that explain how influencer attributes translate into
consumer behavior.



Engagement refers to the degree of cognitive, emotional, and
behavioral interaction with influencer content, including actions
such as liking, commenting, and sharing (Brodie et al., 2013).
While engagement reflects active interaction, prior research
suggests that such interactions do not always translate into
purchase-related outcomes, particularly among Generation
Z users who may engage with content at a superficial level.

Trust represents the consumer’s belief in the reliability,
honesty, and credibility of the influencer (Ohanian, 1990;
Sokolova and Kefi, 2020). Trust functions as a central
mechanism that reduces perceived risk and enhances the
persuasive impact of influencer recommendations. Influencers
perceived as credible and transparent are more likely to
influence purchase intention through trust-based processes.

Perception, in this study, refers to the consumer’s evaluation
of influencer authenticity, credibility, and relevance. This
construct captures how followers interpret influencer content
and assess its alignment with their values and expectations.
From a Parasocial Interaction perspective, perceived authenticity
plays a critical role in shaping these evaluations, as influencers
who appear genuine and relatable are more likely to establish
meaningful connections with their audience (Audrezet et al.,
2020). Consequently, perception functions as a key mediator
linking content-related factors to consumer responses.


2.4 Purchase intention


Purchase intention reflects an individual’s conscious
willingness to purchase a product or service based on available
information and perceived value (Ajzen, 1991). In influencer
marketing contexts, purchase intention is shaped by both
cognitive evaluations (e.g., credibility and trust) and affective
responses (e.g., emotional connection and authenticity). Prior
studies consistently demonstrate that influencer credibility, trust,
and perceived authenticity positively influence purchase
intention, highlighting the importance of psychological
mediators in consumer decision-making.


2.5 Path justification for the conceptual
model


The proposed conceptual model integrates insights from
the Elaboration Likelihood Model (ELM), Source Credibility
Theory (SCT), and Parasocial Interaction Theory (PSI) to explain
how influencer-related factors shape Generation Z consumers’
purchase intention. Rather than assuming multiple simultaneous
pathways, the model emphasizes dominant mediating mechanisms
that are theoretically grounded and empirically supported.


2.5.1 Influencer expertise **→** trust **→** purchase
intention


According to Source Credibility Theory, expertise is a
fundamental determinant of perceived credibility (Ohanian, 1990).
Influencers who demonstrate domain-specific knowledge and
competence are more likely to be perceived as reliable, thereby
enhancing consumer trust. Trust, in turn, reduces perceived risk

and increases the likelihood of accepting influencer

(Sokolova and Kefi, 2020). Thus, trust represents the primary
mechanism through which expertise translates into
consumer behavior.


2.5.2 Influencer passion **→** engagement **→**
purchase intention


Influencer passion reflects emotional intensity and enthusiasm
in content creation, which can stimulate audience interaction.

Drawing on Parasocial Interaction Theory, emotionally
expressive influencers foster a sense of connection that
encourages followers to engage through likes, comments, and
shares (Casaló et al., 2020). However, while engagement reflects
active interaction, its influence on purchase intention may vary,
as Generation Z consumers often engage with content without
necessarily converting that engagement into purchasing behavior.


2.5.3 Influencer passion **→** trust **→** purchase
intention


Beyond engagement, influencer passion may also signal
authenticity. When influencers appear genuinely enthusiastic
rather than commercially motivated, followers are more likely to
perceive them as credible and trustworthy (Audrezet et al.,
2020). This perceived authenticity strengthens trust, which
subsequently influences purchase intention.


2.5.4 Content quality **→** perception **→** purchase
intention


Content quality plays a central role in shaping how influencer
messages are evaluated. Within the ELM framework, high-quality
content enhances message processing and influences consumer
judgments. In this study, perception defined as the consumer’s
evaluation of influencer authenticity, credibility, and relevance
serves as the key mechanism through which content quality affects
purchase intention. High-quality content strengthens positive
perceptions, which in turn influence consumer decision-making.


2.5.5 Frequency of exposure **→** perception **→**
purchase intention


While the mere exposure effect suggests that repeated
exposure increases familiarity (Zajonc, 1968), excessive exposure
in digital environments may lead to diminishing effectiveness.
For Generation Z consumers, frequent exposure may negatively
influence perception by reducing perceived authenticity and
increasing content fatigue. Thus, perception serves as the
primary pathway through which exposure frequency influences
purchase intention.

## 3 Research model and hypothesis development


Drawing on the Elaboration Likelihood Model, Source
Credibility Theory, and Parasocial Interaction Theory, the present



study proposes a conceptual framework to explain how influencer
related cues and content attributes shape Gen Z consumers’
purchase intentions. Influencer expertise, passion, content quality,
and frequency of exposure are expected to affect purchase
intention both directly and indirectly through psychological
mediators such as trust, engagement, and perception. By
integrating these perspectives, the framework (Figure 1) explains
how Gen Z consumers cognitively evaluate influencer messages
and emotionally connect with influencers on social media platform.


3.1 Hypotheses formulation


This study develops a theoretically grounded conceptual
framework that explicates the direct and indirect effects of
influencer and content attributes on purchase intention.
Drawing on the Elaboration Likelihood Model (ELM), influencer
expertise, content quality, and frequency of exposure are
conceptualized as peripheral cues that shape consumer attitudes.
Source Credibility Theory (SCT) grounds the relationships
between expertise, trust, and purchase intention, while
Parasocial Interaction Theory (PSI) explains how passion,
engagement, and perception mediate influencer effectiveness
among Generation Z consumers.


3.1.1 Engagement **→** purchase intention


Engagement reflects the degree of cognitive, emotional, and
behavioral interaction with influencer content. Prior research
suggests that engagement enhances familiarity, perceived social
proof, and emotional attachment, which may strengthen
consumer responses to influencer recommendations (Schivinski
et al., 2016). However, emerging evidence indicates that such
interactions may remain superficial, particularly among
Generation Z consumers, and may not necessarily translate into
purchase intention (Lou and Yuan, 2019).


**H1:** Engagement with influencer content is positively related
to Generation Z consumers’ purchase intention.


3.1.2 Trust **→** purchase intention


Trust represents a critical determinant of persuasion in digital
environments. According to Source Credibility Theory,
consumers are more likely to accept recommendations from
sources perceived as reliable and credible (Ohanian, 1990). Trust
reduces perceived risk and increases confidence in purchase
decisions, thereby strengthening purchase intention (Sokolova
and Kefi, 2020).


**H2:** Trust in influencers is positively related to Generation
Z consumers’ purchase intention.


3.1.3 Perception **→** purchase intention


Perception, defined as the consumer’s evaluation of influencer
authenticity, credibility, and relevance, plays a central role in

## 4 Research methodology


4.1 Research design


This study employed a quantitative cross-sectional survey
design to examine the relationships between influencer-related
factors and Generation Z consumers’ purchase intention.
A structured questionnaire was used to measure the latent
constructs included in the proposed model. The cross-sectional
design enables the analysis of relationships among variables at
a single point in time; however, it does not permit
causal inference.


4.2 Sample and data collection


A purposive non-probability sampling technique was
employed to select respondents who actively engage with
influencer content on social media platforms such as Instagram
and YouTube. This approach is appropriate for targeting



digitally active Generation Z users, who constitute the primary
population of interest in influencer marketing research.

However, the use of purposive sampling introduces limitations
related to external validity and generalizability. The sample is
restricted to respondents from Tier-1 metropolitan cities in India,
who are likely to exhibit higher levels of digital literacy and social
media engagement than the broader population. Therefore, the
findings should be interpreted as context-specific and may not be
generalizable to Generation Z consumers in rural areas, smaller
cities, or different cultural settings. While this sampling approach
enhances contextual relevance, it does not ensure

representativeness of the overall population. Future research may
adopt probability-based sampling techniques or include more
diverse geographic segments to improve generalizability.

The final sample comprised 370 usable responses, which is
considered adequate for the complexity of the proposed
structural model. In line with Hair et al. (2019), SEM studies
with models of moderate complexity generally require a
minimum of 200 observations. Furthermore, applying the
commonly recommended ratio of at least ten observations per
estimated parameter, and considering that the model comprises
eight latent constructs measured by 24 observed indicators, the
sample size of 370 exceeds the minimum requirements for
reliable parameter estimation, thereby providing adequate
statistical power for the proposed structural model.

In addition to direct path estimation, indirect effects were
formally assessed to evaluate the proposed mediation relationships.
Bootstrapping procedures were employed using AMOS 24, with
5,000 bootstrap resamples generated and bias-corrected confidence
intervals computed at the 95% confidence level, consistent with
contemporary recommendations for indirect effect estimation in
SEM-based research (Hair et al., 2019; Preacher and Hayes, 2008).
A mediation effect is considered statistically significant when the
bootstrapped confidence interval does not include zero.


4.3 Instrument development


The measurement instrument (Table 1) was developed using
items adapted from previously validated scales in the influencer
marketing and consumer behavior literature. All constructs were
measured using a five-point Likert scale ranging from 1
(strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). Minor modifications
were made to item wording to ensure contextual relevance to
influencer marketing among Generation Z consumers in India.
The use of established scales supports the content validity and
reliability of the measurement instrument.

measurement instrument prior to the main data collection. The
primary purpose of the pilot study was twofold: to assess the
psychometric properties of the scales and to identify any items

## 6 Discussion of findings


This study provides important insights into how influencer
characteristics are associated with the purchase intentions of

Generation Z consumers in the Indian context. Consistent with

Source Credibility Theory (Ohanian, 1990), the findings indicate
that influencer expertise influences purchase intention indirectly
through trust. For Indian Generation Z consumers, who are
often characterized by skepticism toward overt promotional
content, perceived expertise functions as a credibility cue that
reduces perceived risk and enhances confidence in influencer
recommendation. This finding is particularly relevant in the
Indian digital retail environment, where concerns about
sponsored content may heighten the role of expertise as a trustbuilding signal, and is consistent with prior research conducted
in Western contexts (Casaló et al., 2020), while extending its
applicability to an emerging market setting.

Content quality was also found to influence purchase
intention primarily through perception. High-quality, relevant,
and visually appealing content enhances consumers’ perceptions
of influencer authenticity and alignment with brand values. This
pattern may reflect central-route processing, as described in the
Elaboration Likelihood Model (Petty and Cacioppo, 1986),
where individuals rely on message quality when evaluating
persuasive communication. However, the non-significant role of
engagement as a mediator is particularly noteworthy given that
prior studies conducted in Western contexts have generally
reported positive engagement effects on purchase intention. This
divergence may reflect culturally specific patterns of social media
use among Indian Generation Z consumers, who may engage
with influencer content primarily for entertainment or
information rather than as a precursor to purchase behavior.



Additionally, platform-specific factors and the high volume of
influencer content in the Indian digital ecosystem may
contribute to engagement behaviors that are decoupled from
actual purchasing decisions.

The findings further indicate that frequency of exposure
negatively affects perception and indirectly reduces purchase
intention. This challenges the conventional assumption that
increased exposure enhances persuasion. Instead, the results
may indicate diminishing responsiveness to repeated exposure,
potentially reflecting content fatigue (Reinikainen et al., 2020),
particularly when excessive exposure reduces perceived
authenticity. In contrast, influencer passion demonstrated
only a marginal effect on engagement and no significant
relationship with purchase intention. This finding diverges
from prior studies (Lou and Yuan, 2019) and suggests that
overt expressions of enthusiasm may not consistently translate
into perceived credibility within this context. It is possible
that such expressions are perceived differently within the
Indian cultural context, where audiences may exercise greater
scrutiny toward emotionally expressive promotional
communication; however, this interpretation remains
speculative and warrants further empirical investigation.

Overall, these findings advance theoretical understanding of
influencer marketing by demonstrating that different influencer
attributes operate through distinct psychological mechanisms.
The results further indicate that the effectiveness of influencer
marketing is context-dependent, shaped by the preferences and

behavioral tendencies of Generation Z consumers in

emerging markets.


6.1 Theoretical contributions and insight


First, this study extends the application of Source Credibility
Theory (Ohanian, 1990) and Parasocial Interaction Theory
(Horton and Wohl, 1956) to the context of Indian Generation
Z consumers. While these theories have been widely applied in
Western contexts, their relevance within emerging market
settings, characterized by distinct cultural norms and digital

Second, the findings reveal a negative association between

the observed negative relationship provides a basis for future
research to formally examine this phenomenon as a distinct
construct in influencer marketing contexts.

Third, this study advances the influencer marketing literature
by integrating multiple influencer attributes specifically “expertise
and passion” alongside content-related factors, including content
quality and frequency of exposure, within a unified structural
framework. Unlike prior studies such as Sokolova and Kefi
(2020), which focused primarily on credibility and parasocial
interaction, this study examines the differential mediating roles
of trust and perception in translating influencer attributes into
purchase intention. Notably, while trust and perception emerged
as significant mediators, engagement did not significantly
influence purchase intention in this context. This suggests that
the mediating role of behavioral interaction may be limited
among Generation Z consumers in emerging markets.


6.2 Practical and managerial implications


For marketers, brand strategists, and content creators
operating in lifestyle-oriented sectors, the findings suggest that
strategic emphasis should be placed on domain expertise,
credibility, and content quality. Collaborations should prioritize
influencers who are not only popular but also perceived as
knowledgeable and authentic within their niche.

In addition, brands should carefully manage content
frequency. Rather than relying on volume, a strategically timed
and curated approach is more likely to sustain consumer interest
and trust. Overexposure may lead to disengagement, thereby
undermining campaign effectiveness. Maintaining a balance
between visibility and value is crucial for long-term impact.

The findings carry several specific practical implications for
marketers, influencer agencies, and content creators:


  - Prioritize Expertise to Build Trust: Brands targeting Indian
Generation Z should collaborate with influencers who
demonstrate clear domain knowledge and professional
credibility, as trust emerged as a significant and
influential mediator of purchase intention.

  - Focus on Quality over Quantity: High-quality, relevant content
enhances consumer perception and indirectly strengthens
purchase intention more effectively than frequent posting.

  - Avoid Overexposure: Excessive posting frequency may
contribute to content fatigue and diminish brand impact;
optimal content schedules should balance visibility with
perceived novelty and value.




  - Rethink Engagement Metrics: High engagement rates do
not necessarily translate into higher purchase intentions;
marketers should evaluate the quality of interactions
rather than their quantity.”

## 7 Conclusion


This study developed and tested a structural model examining
the relationships between influencer attributes, content quality,
and Generation Z consumers’ purchase intention in the Indian
digital retail context. The findings indicate that trust and
perception emerged as significant determinants of purchase
intention, operating as key psychological mechanisms through
which influencer expertise and content quality translate into
consumer behavior. In contrast, engagement did not significantly
influence purchase intention, and frequency of exposure
demonstrated a negative association with perception, which may
indicate the presence of content fatigue or diminishing
responsiveness to repeated influencer content among Generation
Z consumers. These findings highlight the relative importance of
trust and perception as the primary mechanisms linking
influencer attributes to purchase intention, while suggesting that
behavioral engagement metrics may be limited predictors of
actual consumer decision-making in this context.

From a theoretical standpoint, this study extends the
application of Source Credibility Theory and Parasocial
Interaction Theory to the Indian Generation Z context,
providing empirical evidence of their relevance within an
emerging market setting. By integrating multiple influencer
attributes and content-related factors within a unified structural
framework, the study advances understanding of how distinct
influencer characteristics operate through different psychological
pathways to influence consumer behavior.

For practitioners, the findings suggest that influencer strategies
should prioritize credibility, authenticity, and content quality over
excessive exposure frequency. Brands targeting Generation
Z consumers in India are advised to collaborate with influencers
who demonstrate domain-specific expertise and produce highquality, relevant content, rather than relying on high posting
frequency to sustain consumer interest. These insights are
especially relevant in the current digital ecosystem, where
Generation Z consumers exercise considerable selectivity in their
engagement with influencer content.

Overall, this study contributes a theoretically grounded and
empirically validated understanding of influencer marketing
effectiveness among Generation Z consumers in India, offering a
foundation for future research to further examine the contextual

and cultural factors that shape influencer-driven consumer
behavior in emerging markets.”

## 8 Limitations and future research


As with most empirical studies, this research is subject to
several limitations that should be considered when interpreting
the findings.

First, the study employs purposive non-probability sampling
confined to Tier-1 metropolitan cities in India, which restricts the

generalizability of the findings. The sample primarily represents
digitally active urban consumers; therefore, the results may not be
applicable to Generation Z populations in rural areas or different
socio-economic contexts. Future research should adopt
probability-based sampling techniques and include more
geographically diverse segments to improve representativeness.

Second, the use of a cross-sectional research design limits the
ability to establish causal relationships among variables. While the
model assumes directional relationships, alternative explanations
cannot be ruled out. For instance, it is equally plausible that preexisting trust influences perceptions of influencer expertise,
indicating the possibility of reverse causality. Future research may
adopt longitudinal or experimental designs to address this limitation.

Third, as all variables were measured using self-reported data
collected at a single point in time, the study is susceptible to
common method bias (CMB). Although procedural remedies
such as ensuring respondent anonymity were applied, no formal
statistical tests (e.g., Harman’s single-factor test) were conducted
to assess the extent of CMB. Future studies are encouraged to
incorporate both procedural and statistical techniques to
mitigate this issue.

Fourth, while trust and perception were modeled as distinct
constructs, there is potential conceptual overlap between them,
which may raise concerns regarding discriminant validity.
Although statistical tests support construct validity, future
research could further refine the operationalization of these
constructs to ensure clearer conceptual separation.

Fifth, the study focuses on influencer marketing within the
fashion and food sectors, primarily on platforms such as
Instagram and YouTube. This limits the applicability of the
findings to other industries and platforms, where influencer
dynamics and user engagement patterns may differ
substantially. Future research could extend the model to
alternative domains to enhance external validity. Furthermore,
the weak effect of influencer passion on engagement and
purchase intention may reflect platform-specific and
expression-related factors not captured in the current study.
Future research could examine how different modes of

emotional expression across platforms such as Instagram Reels
and YouTube Shorts influence the persuasive effectiveness of
influencer passion among Generation Z consumers.

Sixth, while quantitative methods have provided structural
insights into influencer marketing effectiveness, future research
could employ qualitative approaches — such as in-depth
interviews or case studies — to explore the subjective
dimensions of influencer authenticity and content fatigue in
greater depth, providing richer contextual understanding of the
psychological mechanisms identified in this study.

Finally, the findings are situated within the Indian cultural
context, which may influence consumer perceptions and
responses to influencer marketing. Future research could adopt
cross-cultural comparative approaches, including multinational
comparative studies, to examine how socio-cultural factors shape
the effectiveness of influencer attributes and mediating
mechanisms, thereby enhancing the generalizability of the findings.

## Appendix A: Survey Instrument Scales

All items were measured on a 5-point Likert scale
(1 = Strongly Disagree to 5 = Strongly Agree).


  - Independent Variables


**Influencer Expertise (IFE)** (Adapted from Casaló et al., 2020;
Ohanian, 1990)


  - **IFE1:** The influencer is highly knowledgeable in

their domain.

  - **IFE2:** The influencer provides reliable and
accurate information.

  - **IFE3:** The influencer is experienced in food/

fashion content.


**Influencer Passion (IFP)** (Adapted from Lou and Yuan, 2019)


  - **IFP1:** The influencer shows genuine passion for what
they promote.

  - **IFP2:** The influencer appears enthusiastic and invested in
their content.

  - **IFP3:** Influencer seems emotionally connected to

their niche.


**Content Quality (CQ)** (Adapted from Djafarova and Trofimenko,
2019; Hwang and Zhang, 2018)


  - **CQ1:** The content is visually appealing and
professionally produced.

  - **CQ2:** The influencer shares high-quality and creative posts.

  - **CQ3:** The content is consistent and relevant to my interests.


**Frequency of Exposure (FOE)** (Adapted from Reinikainen et al.,
2020)


  - **FOE1:** I frequently see this influencer’s posts on my feed.




  - **FOE2:** The influencer posts content regularly.

  - **FOE3:** I often engage with the influencer’s posts (likes,
comments, shares).

  - Mediating Variables


**Engagement (ENG)** (Adapted from Jin et al., 2019)


  - **ENG1:** I like, comment, or share this influencer’s
content regularly.

  - **ENG2:** I actively follow updates from this influencer.

  - **ENG3:** I enjoy interacting with the influencer’s content.


**Trust (TT)** (Adapted from Ohanian, 1990; Sokolova and Kefi,
2020)


  - **TT1:** I trust the information shared by this influencer.

  - **TT2:** The influencer appears honest and transparent.

  - **TT3:** I believe this influencer has my best interests in mind.


**Perception (PP)** (Adapted from Audrezet et al., 2020)


  - **PP1:** I can relate to this influencer personally.

  - **PP2:** The influencer reflects values and lifestyle similar

to mine.

  - **PP3:** I view the influencer as authentic and genuine.

  - Dependent Variable


**Purchase Intention (PI)** (Adapted from Sokolova and Kefi, 2020)


  - **PI1:** I would consider buying a product recommended by
this influencer.

  - **PI2:** I have already bought products based on this
influencer’s suggestion.

  - **PI3:** I am likely to try a product if this influencer promotes

it.
