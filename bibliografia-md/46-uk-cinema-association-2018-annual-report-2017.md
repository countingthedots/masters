###### Introduction



**2017 was another strong year for the UK**

**cinema sector.**


The cinema-going public again responded to a
diverse film slate and the continued investment by
cinema operators in all aspects of the big screen
experience such that 2017 out-performed the
preceding year in terms of both box office and
admissions, the former setting a new UK record.


Film distribution colleagues again played a major role
here, continuing to observe a 12 months, 52 weeks
of the year release strategy, with films from every
quarter of 2017 eventually occupying the final year’s

top ten.


But also key were unprecedented levels of

investment in the UK sector, with the dividend

provided by digitisation and the continuing
emergence of ‘high end’ business models in
particular resulting in a significant number of
refurbishments and new-builds emerging during
the year (and planned for years to come).


All of the above provided further evidence both of
the health of the UK cinema sector and its ongoing

resilience in the face of economic and other

commercial challenges.


While preparations for ‘Brexit’ continued to take up
a good deal of the attention of key politicians and
policy-makers, there were nevertheless a range of
opportunities in 2017 for the Association to reinforce

with that same constituency its core message

around the economic, social and cultural value of the

UK cinema sector.



During the year, the Association continued to work
with its members and colleagues from across the
industry on a wide range of strategic and operational

issues.


As in previous years, a significant amount of

Association time was spent on issues around
disability and access, with the aim of maintaining and
enhancing the reputation of the UK sector as a World

leader in accessibility.


Efforts to maintain the security of film content also
continued to enjoy great success, with no recorded
instances in 2017 of films being recorded in a UK

cinema.


A well-received conference on data and analytics
saw the establishment of an exercise to identify a
preferred partner for a collective deal for smaller

operator members in this area.


2017 saw the _Meerkat Movies_ 2 for 1 cinema ticket

promotion continue to grow, providing further
evidence of its value in driving incremental cinema
admissions for operators of all sizes.


The Association remains dependent on – and
hugely grateful for – the support and engagement
of both its Executive Board, its wider membership

and partners across the industry as it endeavours

to support cinema operators large and small as they

endeavour to ensure that as many people as possible
choose to see films in their best possible setting –

the cinema theatre.

#### 2.2 The Association strongly supports the principle of maintaining a clear ‘window’

between a film’s theatrical release and its release

on other platforms, believing that this ensures that

audiences continue to enjoy the widest possible
range of films in their intended environment, the
cinema theatre. It is the unique nature of the cinema

experience that means that so many
cinemas across the UK remain key focal points for

their local communities.

During the course of 2017, the average
#### 2.3 theatrical release window increased from

102 to just over 110 days. As ever however, this
masked a wide range of practices, with a number of
films being released on very short windows of less

than two weeks and an increasing number observing
windows of 30-90 days.

#### 2.4 While decisions on the release window around any individual film are a

commercial matter between exhibitors and the

relevant distributor, and not therefore something

in which the Association has any role, it would be

concerned by any moves taken in this regard which
were at the expense of cinema exhibition, a part
of the film industry which continues to perform
strongly and provide a solid foundation
for subsequent elements of film revenue.

#### 2.5 As has been the case in recent years, 2017 saw a small number of attempts from

outside of the industry to ‘disrupt’ the current
business model, typically through the promotion of

an early video-on-demand service. While in the end
none of these came to fruition, dialogue continued
_within_ the industry (and on a commercially
confidential basis) on how best to meet the needs

of the customer while maintaining economic viability.
